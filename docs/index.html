A gene-expression prognostic score for diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, developed and validated on public data

Meriç Arda GürbüzOctober 2026Code and resultsFull reportLocked analysis plan

This is a research project, not a diagnostic or treatment tool. The score is standardised within each cohort, so it cannot be applied to an individual new patient, and nothing on this page supports a clinical decision.

Abstract

Background. Outcome after R-CHOP varies widely in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL). The International Prognostic Index (IPI) and cell of origin (COO) capture part of this variation. We asked whether a gene-expression score adds to them, using only public data and a pre-specified analysis.

1Study design

GSE181063 (Illumina DASL, formalin-fixed tissue) was split 70/30 before any analysis. All modelling decisions, including gene selection and the penalty, were made on the training part. The analysis plan, with its hypotheses and conclusion sentences, was written and hash-locked before the test set was opened. The test part and two Affymetrix cohorts, GSE10846 and GSE87371, were then opened once each, in that order.

2Validation

The frozen 44 genes and coefficients were applied unchanged to every held-out cohort. In the external cohorts only the scale was re-estimated, by z-scoring each gene within the cohort, because the platforms differ. The score was associated with overall survival in all three.

Note. All three validation C-indices are slightly above the training estimate, by 0.02 to 0.05. Every interval contains 0.61, and the sets have only 41 to 74 deaths, so chance is the simplest explanation. The intervals matter more than the point values.

3Survival by risk group

In the test set, patients were split at the training median of the score. In the external cohorts each cohort's own median was used, because absolute score values do not transfer between platforms. Tertiles are shown for description only.

4Negative control

To check that the result is not an artefact of the pipeline, the whole procedure, including the cross-validated choice of penalty, was run 100 times on training data whose survival times and events had been shuffled together. Each of these permutation models was scored on the real test labels.

5Comparison with IPI and COO

The question that matters clinically is whether the score adds anything to what IPI and COO already say. Cox models with IPI (0–5, linear) and COO (GCB, ABC, other) were fitted with and without the score. These analyses use complete cases only, so patient numbers are smaller than in Section 2.

6Biological interpretation

This analysis is exploratory and uses the training set only; no hypothesis was tested and p-values are descriptive. Higher scores go with proliferation, MYC targets and the ABC/IRF4 programme. Lower scores go with extracellular-matrix, stromal and T-cell genes. This is the pattern described by Rosenwald et al. [1] and Lenz et al. [2], so the score mostly rediscovers known DLBCL biology.

An unresolved contradiction. Lenz et al. linked the stromal-2 (angiogenesis) signature to poor outcome, but our angiogenesis proxy goes with the low-risk side. The proxy overlaps with matrix genes, so stromal-1 and stromal-2 cannot be separated here. Six signatures together explain about 35% of the variance of the score; the rest is not explained by them.

7Model genes

Only 4 of the 44 genes were selected in at least half of 100 bootstrap refits. The list is one of many similar sparse solutions; what was validated is the score as a whole, not the individual genes.

8Limitations

  • No clinical use. Within-cohort z-scoring means absolute risk and cut-offs do not transfer. There is no external calibration and no decision-curve analysis.
  • The "adds information" label rests on a weak external condition. It required only a point estimate above 1 in the external cohorts. Both intervals include 1 and both likelihood-ratio tests are non-significant.
  • The internal test set shares cohort, platform, laboratory and COO classifier with training. The added-value hypothesis was met only there.
  • Missing IPI. Complete-case analysis drops 36 test and 75 GSE10846 patients. In the test set, patients with missing IPI died more often (55.6% vs 42.9%, log-rank p = 0.074).
  • Small external cohorts. GSE87371 has 41 deaths, and excluding 13 R-mini-CHOP21 patients moves its HR to 1.39 (0.98–1.96). GSE10846 has a median follow-up of 34 months.
  • Processed GEO values were used, not re-processed CEL files, and pre-processing differs between cohorts.
  • The pre-registration is internal. The plan was hash-locked before the test set was opened but was not deposited with an external registry, so the order of events is documented, not independently provable.
  • Modest novelty. The contribution is a transparent, fully logged workflow, not new biology. No head-to-head comparison with published DLBCL signatures was done.
